# Native128 versus FI64: why it wins and distance to SoL

The long-sequence native128 kernel reaches about 85% of NCU's sustained
**dense BF16 tensor-operation peak at the profiling clock**. This is not a
claim of 100% attention SoL, nor a claim covering all shapes. Short sequences
are substantially farther from the compute roof and can lose to FI64.

This audit profiles the unchanged C4 GPU kernel and FI commit
`f75bed5da7112103b75f82a4d4b5faf4be335154` on SM120, 110 SMs, Nsight Compute
2025.3.1. Inputs: B=1, D=128, BF16, logical128 blocks, two dense prefix blocks
and independent 10% selections from the two remaining regions, seed42.
`harness/profile_kernel.py` profiles both providers on identical tensors.
`reports/full.ncu-rep` contains full, PM-sampling and hierarchical tensor
roofline sections; `reports/source.ncu-rep` has CuTe line information.
`analysis/sol.json`, `derived_sol.json`, `raw_*.json`, `stall_*.txt` and
`pm_timeline_plots.txt` were extracted with the `ncu_report` API and pinned
KDA helpers. The 32K/H14 row is reused from the retained C4/FI full reports.

## Direct SoL evidence

The metric is
`sm__ops_path_tensor_op_hmma_src_bf16_dst_fp32_sparsity_off.avg.pct_of_peak_sustained_elapsed`,
not merely an activity proxy. Its values happen to equal tensor-pipe elapsed
activity for these kernels. The `sparsity_off` peak is correct: block-sparse
attention does not use the hardware's 2:4 structured-sparsity doubling.

| S / heads | FI BF16 compute SoL | Native128 BF16 compute SoL | FI SM busy | Native SM busy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1024 / 14 | 44.20% | 41.41% | 66.47% | 63.43% |
| 8192 / 14 | 63.02% | 75.67% | 78.04% | 93.86% |
| 32768 / 14 | 76.89% | 84.66% | 91.65% | 98.67% |
| 32768 / 56 | 82.25% | 85.54% | 97.69% | 99.53% |

SM busy is `sm__cycles_active.avg / sm__cycles_elapsed.avg`; it is distinct
from warp occupancy. For 8K, tensor activity during active SM cycles is
80.74% FI versus 80.62% native. Most of the whole-kernel difference is
therefore explained by the improved active-SM fraction, rather than faster
MMA execution within already-active cycles. For 32K/H14, the corresponding
active tensor rates are 83.90% versus 85.81%, with a further improvement in
the active-SM fraction. This refines the earlier broad scheduling diagnosis.

## Why native128 is faster on these long inputs

Both kernels execute exactly the same tensor work. For 32K/H14,
106,736 selected logical edges × 128 × 128 × (4 × 128) =
**895,366,463,488 operations**. NCU's executed BF16 operation counter equals
that number for both providers. The other three shapes also match their
independently computed operation counts exactly. No precision reduction,
token dropping, or structured-sparsity instruction changes are involved.

FI handles each logical128 edge through four physical64 tiles. Native128
uses a physical128 tile, so K/V loaded for one 128-row Q tile can serve all
those queries. The code still uses SM120 `mma.sync` and the FI-derived TMA
and staged register-fragment machinery; 128 is the software tile size,
not a new hardware MMA instruction.

For 32K/H14, NCU shows:

| Counter | FI64 | Native128 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Global-to-shared TMA bytes | 14,107,541,504 | 7,112,491,008 |
| TMA load instructions | 1,722,112 | 434,112 |
| Ordinary global-load instructions | 3,871,168 | 1,843,184 |
| L2 throughput / sustained peak | 50.19% | 28.01% |
| DRAM throughput / sustained peak | 6.56% | 12.40% |
| L2 hit rate | 96.61% | 88.74% |
| Registers / thread | 254 | 241 |
| Achieved warp occupancy | 16.45% | 16.61% |

The TMA byte reduction is real, but it is **not** a halving of DRAM traffic.
Native's cache hit rate is lower and its DRAM throughput is higher; DRAM is
still far from saturated. Grouped query/head scheduling starts heavy dense
prefix rows earlier and reduces the final idle portion. Fewer transfers and
metadata/loop operations help keep the tensor work supplied. These are
combined-design comparisons, not a new isolated ablation of each change.

## How much room remains

At the recorded ~1.738 GHz profiling clock, NCU reports a BF16 tensor peak
of 195.74 TFLOP/s for this device. Native32K/H14 achieves 165.72 TFLOP/s;
FI achieves 150.49 TFLOP/s. A compute-only lower bound is therefore:

`895.366 GFLOP / 195.742 TFLOP/s = 4.574 ms`.

Native's profiled duration is 5.403 ms. Reaching that idealized lower bound
would mean at most **1.181× additional speedup**, or **15.34% lower time**,
at the same clock, operation count and arithmetic path. Softmax, reductions,
data movement and dependencies still cost time, so this is not a promise
that the remaining gap is attainable. It also does not bound designs that
change precision or required work. NCU timings use replay/cache and clock
policies; standalone paired timings establish application-facing speedups.

## Six-dimension diagnosis and next priorities

1. **Grid/occupancy:** 32K/H14 has 32.58 CTA waves/SM in both designs. Native
   uses one 256-thread CTA/SM; FI uses two 128-thread CTAs. Both expose about
   eight active warps/SM. Increasing occupancy alone is not the explanation
   for the win. 1K lacks enough sustained work and loses to FI.
2. **Balance:** the active-SM fractions above expose the largest 8K gain.
   At 32K/H14 native's max active cycles exceed its SM mean by only 1.22%.
   Retained PM samples support examining the tail, with missing PM metrics
   explicitly marked rather than substituted.
3. **Stalls:** source attribution places the largest native hotspots at
   QK MMA line739 and PV MMA line766, followed by operand loads line763.
   For 32K/H14, math-pipe throttle is 46.30%, wait 27.59%, and long-scoreboard
   1.35% of PC samples. These are sampled warp stall proportions, not additive
   fractions of wall time and not direct recoverable speedups.
4. **Tensor throughput:** long-sequence 84.66–85.54% is close to the tensor
   roof, but 8K 75.67% and 1K 41.41% clearly are not universal saturation.
5. **Memory:** DRAM saturation does not explain the long-shape limit.
   Zero local-memory spill instructions were measured. Preserve the reduced
   TMA traffic while examining instruction/dependency overlap.
6. **Generalization:** the expanded standalone matrix finds regressions for
   H=1, 1% random density, single-selected-block and ragged blocks, and near
   the 16K scheduler threshold. Keep opt-in; do not globally dispatch this
   kernel on the basis of the favorable 32K SoL measurement.

The highest-value next optimization is shape/density-aware scheduling and
small-workload handling, using new tuning cases and separate holdouts.
For the already-compute-efficient long dense-enough cases, investigate MMA /
softmax overlap only after an isolated profiler-backed experiment. Full H3
activation/video quality and end-to-end inference remain unverified.

Metric interpretation follows NVIDIA's
[Profiling Guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-compute/ProfilingGuide/index.html)
and [peak-metric clarification](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-are-pct-of-peak-metrics-calculated/330187).
