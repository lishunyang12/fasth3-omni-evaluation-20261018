FastH3: Precision and
Decoder Ablations

We evaluate reference-conditioned video-and-audio generation across checkpoint precisions and video decoders. Matched prompts, reference inputs, and random seeds support qualitative comparisons against the original BF16 model. Latency and memory are measured on four NVIDIA RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell GPUs.

Experimental setup. Four 72 GB GPUs connected through PCIe; Ulysses degree 4; text-encoder tensor parallelism 4; VAE patch parallelism 4. Eager execution, no CPU offloading, and one request at a time. Generated media retain their original colors and audio.

All three measured outputs per configuration are fully decoded and hashed. Per-configuration repeatability is recorded in the downloadable media validation data.

Frame-aligned differences between checkpoints may reflect different motion or composition. They are not perceptual quality scores.

Sparsity and quantized attention
Single-GPU replay


We replay inputs captured at three denoising-step and transformer-layer pairs from the four-GPU original BF16 run, using one Ulysses rank on one RTX PRO 5000. VSA timing includes layout conversion, pooling, sparse routing, learned compression, and output processing. The quantized path additionally includes Q/K INT8 and V FP8 quantization on every call.

Dense attention and VSA have different semantics; the quantized path preserves VSA routing while changing fine-attention arithmetic. Relative L2 is measured against cuDNN BF16 VSA: CAKE differs by 1.70%, 12.82%, and 13.48% at the three sampled step–layer pairs. Kernel provenance
FastVideo uses its portable BF16 Triton block-sparse kernel. NVIDIA cuDNN uses its SM120 tile128 CuTe DSL kernel for BF16 block-sparse attention. The FI configuration invokes the SM120 CAKE Sage kernel explicitly, with INT8 Q/K and FP8 V quantization inside every timed call. Logical tile128 routing and learned compression are retained. Source revisions, measured inputs, and per-rank invocation evidence are included in the downloadable provenance.
