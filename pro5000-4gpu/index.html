We evaluate reference-conditioned video-and-audio generation across checkpoint precisions and video decoders. Matched prompts, reference inputs, and random seeds support qualitative comparisons against the original BF16 model. Latency and memory are measured on four NVIDIA RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell GPUs.

Experimental setup. Four 72 GB GPUs connected through PCIe; Ulysses degree 4; text-encoder tensor parallelism 4; VAE patch parallelism 4. Eager execution, no CPU offloading, and one request at a time. Generated media retain their original colors and audio.

Measurement protocol and scope
Output
1344 × 768 pixels, 124 frames at 24 fps, eight denoising steps, and generated stereo audio.
Timing
Client request to complete MP4 response, excluding model loading and warmup. End-to-end and individual stage medians are computed separately. The range below each end-to-end median gives the minimum and maximum of the three requests.
Repeatability
All three measured outputs per completed configuration were fully decoded; video pixels and audio match within each configuration.
Interpretation
Frame-aligned differences between checkpoints may reflect different motion or composition. They are not perceptual quality scores.
Coverage
The completed results currently cover BF16 and NVFP4 with single-image conditioning. FP8, multi-image conditioning, and video references will be included after their runs complete. No single-GPU or end-to-end Sage result is inferred from this table.

Sparsity and quantized attention
Single-GPU replay


We replay inputs captured at three denoising-step and transformer-layer pairs from the four-GPU NVFP4 run, using one Ulysses rank on one RTX PRO 5000. VSA timing includes layout conversion, pooling, sparse routing, learned compression, and output processing. The quantized path additionally includes Q/K INT8 and V FP8 quantization on every call.

End-to-end video quality with Sage has not yet been evaluated.


Kernel provenance
BF16 VSA uses FlashInfer's SM120 block-sparse CuTe DSL kernel. The Sage replay uses CAKE-generated SM120 CUDA kernels from a separate FlashInfer source checkout, with Triton operand quantization in vLLM-Omni. It does not call the upstream sageattention.sageattn() entry point. Full video generation uses the installed FlashInfer 0.7.0.post1 package; the replay checkout is recorded in the downloadable provenance.
