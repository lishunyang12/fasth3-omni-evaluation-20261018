Resource residency and instruction work

CAKE uses fewer registers and less shared memory, allowing three resident blocks per SM and a higher achieved occupancy. Across the three captures, it executes 20.1–20.2% fewer warp instructions, while Tensor pipeline activity rises from approximately 68% to 79%. DRAM activity is approximately 3% of peak and the L2 hit rate exceeds 98.6%. These observations support resource residency and instruction work as contributors to the measured latency difference; the counters do not indicate DRAM bandwidth saturation. Neither kernel shows local spilling requests.

Connection to the implementation

The cuDNN shared-memory structure allocates separate Q, K, V, and output buffers. The CAKE kernel reuses Q/K staging storage for the output, consistent with its lower shared-memory requirement. Scale handling also differs: cuDNN uses per-row Q scales and K scales per 16 keys; CAKE uses Q scales per 32 rows and K scales per 64 keys. Together with the different Q/K formats, these are plausible contributors to instruction-count differences. Their individual performance effects have not been isolated by ablation.

Profiling used Nsight Compute 2025.3.1, five warmup calls per provider, and 19 kernel replay passes per capture and provider. Quantization and allocation were outside the profiling range. Clocks were unlocked, caches were not flushed, and the device remained shared; concurrent activity and replay variation cannot be excluded. Profiled native-call durations in the download are separate from the CUDA Graph medians in Table 1. No source-PC stall attribution was collected. See NVIDIA’s profiling guide for counter and replay interpretation.

4 / METHOD AND PROVENANCE

Controlled inputs and timing scope

Inputs were captured at step/layer 0/0, 3/49, and 7/49 from the original BF16 FastH3 checkpoint. The original attention replay was verified against each stored output before extracting fine-attention operands. Logical tile128 selection is lowered to tile64 metadata while preserving selected valid tokens, variable block counts, and partial key/value blocks. Sparse routing and learned compression are outside the measured region.

Each implementation receives five compilation/warmup calls, followed by five graph warmups and 30 timed graph replays. Provider order rotates between repetitions. Both implementations retain native behavior, including cuDNN LSE writes. Small masked-attention checks cover variable counts and partial/padded blocks before measurements. Raw samples and numerical diagnostics are available below.

The cuDNN blk64 BSA kernel is from frontend 1.31.0. Its original quantizer failed to compile on SM120 because it emitted an unsupported FP32 redux instruction. The benchmark uses NVIDIA’s official upstream SM120 quantizer fix; the blk64 BSA source at that revision is byte-identical to 1.31.0. CAKE uses the explicit backend="cake" SM120 entry point and the vLLM-Omni Triton Q/K/V quantizers.
