What to inspect: hand and foot contours during fast motion, coherent transitions between poses, continuity of the face and uniform, and moving sleeve and belt folds. Use the shared frame slider or slow playback to inspect corresponding times.

Controlled settings: all three clips were newly generated with the same prompt, two reference images, seed, sampler, single GPU and layerwise CPU-offload configuration. All use the same original BF16 encoder, cuDNN BF16 attention and original BF16 video/audio VAE. QAD uses its published DiT weights; its quantized encoder is not used here.

Direct Online NVFP4: untouched BF16 source weights use round-to-nearest NVFP4 with weight tensor amax. Every DiT projection computes its activation global scale from its current input amax; per-16 E4M3 scales are dynamic. No calibration table, QAD weights, QAD training or learned QAD numerical artifacts are used in that arm.

*Timing: one first request after each model starts, including CPU offload, possible first-use JIT, generation, encoding and receipt. Model loading and offline weight packing are excluded. These are not the previous four-GPU production measurements and do not establish quantization speedup.

Metrics: full-resolution decoded frames, aligned by frame index; all 124 frames are averaged. Lower LPIPS and higher SSIM mean closer to this BF16 reference. A static dark background contributes to the scores. These metrics alone do not assess action correctness, temporal stability, audio or general video quality; trajectories can diverge despite the same seed.

Prompt and reference inputs
Use <Picture 1> and <Picture 2> as two views of the same martial artist. Preserve his face, short dark hair, white karate uniform and black belt. Exactly one person performs in the same dark studio.

Goal and scene: a five-second realistic martial-arts action shot with clearly resolved hands, feet, cloth folds and natural motion. Show his entire body with enough space for a side kick. Preserve the soft side lighting and dark background of the references.
0–1 s: From a balanced guard facing three-quarters right, he steps forward and throws one controlled straight punch, then retracts the fist. His white sleeve snaps and settles.
1–2 s: He throws a second straight punch with the other hand and returns both hands to guard. His shoulders and hips rotate together; the belt ends swing with his motion.
2–3.5 s: He pivots on his left foot, raises the right knee, extends one waist-height right side kick toward screen right, and retracts the leg. His supporting leg stays planted and his hands remain in guard.
3.5–5 s: He places the right foot down, rotates his torso back toward the opening direction and settles into a stable guard. The sleeves and belt ends continue moving briefly and then settle.
Camera: one uninterrupted full-body shot; move slowly sideways through a small arc while keeping the person centered. No cuts or zooms. Keep the kick and both hands inside the frame.
Sound: foot contact, short breaths and crisp cloth movement synchronized with the punches and kick; no speech or music.
Continuity: retain one face, one body, the same clothing and the same studio throughout. Maintain connected wrists, elbows, knees and ankles during the transitions. No extra people, duplicated limbs, captions or overlays.
