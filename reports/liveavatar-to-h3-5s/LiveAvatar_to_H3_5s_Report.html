02 / Research → implementation

Which LiveAvatar ideas reached H3?

LiveAvatar studies long-running, audio-driven generation using a causal few-step model, noisy historical KV and timestep pipeline scheduling. We adapted inference mechanisms to H3; H3’s current target block remained bidirectional, and we did not reproduce LiveAvatar’s training. Paper, v6 · Official implementation.

03 / Quality screening

Useful signals, with a limited paired sample

The first screen used 4 GPUs, SP4 / encoder TP4 / VAE PP4, four-step MXFP8/Sage, full video VAE and 73-frame windows with 68 new frames. The three complete paired units were Nora idle at two seeds and Nora speech at one seed, all 576 × 1024. Each branch was approximately 181.54 s.

Coverage matters: the study stopped early at the user’s request. It included 1,636 clips and 25 complete scored trajectories out of 56 planned; only 3 of 8 planned scene/seed groups had all seven arms. Another 52 completed clips were excluded by the cutoff. We use complete paired groups for the aggregate comparison.
Per-step KV follow-up

Appearance improved; motion still mattered

Compared with the prefix-off control, mean DINO similarity changed from 0.9652 → 0.9725 for two idle seeds, and 0.9422 → 0.9504 for one speech seed.

The speech temporal score slightly decreased, and aesthetics decreased. This supports further continuity work, not an overall quality-win claim.

Anchor follow-up

AAS did not help the combined stack

With matched KV, full overlap reuse and native VAE blending already enabled, AAS lowered DINO by 0.0071, increased mean join RGB change by 2.39%, and improved only 20 of 63 joins.

One speech scene, one seed, 181.54 s per arm. Selected configuration kept AAS disabled.

04 / Continuity results

The improvements came from preserving the right state

Matched historical KV alone did not remove visible segment boundaries. We then tested overlap state reuse and decoder continuity on a 31.375 s speech sequence, 576 × 1024 at 24 FPS, four GPUs.

1. Reuse the full overlap trajectory

Retain two overlapping video latents across all four noisy states plus the final clean state. Mean join change fell 5.795 → 3.501 (39.6%); DINO remained close, 0.95513 → 0.95538.

Reusing only the initial overlap noise did not help: 5.906. This hard timestep-matched overlap constraint is different from simply disabling the old clean prefix.

2. Preserve native VAE blending

Decode the same generated latents with continuous temporal blending. Mean join change fell 3.431 → 2.774 (19.1%); all 10 joins improved, and local neighborhood peaks fell 17.5%.

Decoded audio was identical. DINO was nearly unchanged: 0.963758 → 0.963791. This redistributes a boundary transition; it cannot fix a generated pose reset.

Follow-up refinements that did not establish a better default

Extending the blend endpoint to 1 reduced mean join change by only 0.63%, with no established perceptual benefit. Changing the continuation prompt increased DINO by 0.0030 but worsened join change by 5.06%. The retained choice was native continuous blending. Blend and prompt treatments were tested independently, not as a combined arm.

Single scene/seed short screens. The decode experiment was offline at this stage; later pipeline/browser prototypes have separate qualification records.

Later browser prototype · 17 October · separate workload

The later native-v9 prototype used 6 DiT GPUs + 1 codec GPU, with a separate voice GPU, at 480 × 864, 17 new frames, four steps. A 312.33 s browser test recorded 24.003 generated FPS and 23.408 presented FPS, with 11 stalls / 3.840 s total. Three input-end-to-present samples were 6.015–6.946 s; interruption was 625.8 ms.

This was a browser on the inference host accessing the public tunnel, using a synthetic microphone. It was historically deployed as a prototype; current availability is not asserted. Quality qualification, one-second conversation response and physical acoustic echo cancellation were not established. These numbers do not replace the 8-GPU landscape 5s baseline.

07 / What the results support

What to carry into the next five-second demo

Retain for reproduction

The measured 5s recipe

Keep the pinned 960 × 544, 124/119-frame, four-step reference for apples-to-apples latency comparisons. Its 3.706 s ready median is historical evidence.

Candidates for a matched integration

Continuity state + decode

Evaluate matched historical KV, full overlap trajectory and native continuous VAE blending together on the exact five-second workload. The recorded follow-ups establish feasibility, not a new 5s latency result.

Keep as explicit tradeoffs

Anchors, resolution and pipelines

Keep AAS off under the retained combined-stack result. Keep window-relative positions. Treat lower resolution, larger blocks and pipeline depth as quality/throughput/response-latency decisions.

The report-ready claim: the historical 5s avatar ran faster than its content duration; later LiveAvatar-inspired H3 experiments improved selected continuity diagnostics and enabled pipelined streaming, with separate configurations and clear latency tradeoffs.

Evidence boundaries and reading notes
  • The report packages historical records; it introduces no new model-generation benchmark.
  • The 5s reference, 4-GPU quality studies, 5-GPU pipeline trials and later browser prototype have distinct inputs, resolutions and timing scopes. Their speed ratios are not interchangeable.
  • The earlier avatar quality page is a separate 243-frame / 10.125 s experiment. Its latency must not be presented as the 5s measurement.
  • The original quality study did not finish its full planned matrix. Negative results and untested deployment combinations remain explicit.
  • Paired controls froze identical encoded reference-audio conditions after uncontrolled re-encoding failed repeat qualification. Four-GPU controlled repeats were exact; equivalence to earlier eight-GPU historical outputs was not assumed.
  • Published video assets are retained experimental outputs copied without re-encoding. Their hashes are listed separately. No replacement video is presented as the missing September clips.
  • Model-based quality metrics and seam statistics are diagnostics. They do not establish identity preservation, speech correctness, human preference or lip synchronization.
  • The source manifest identifies the original local records by filename and hash. Only selected result fields and three videos are included here; this is not a complete public reproduction package for every October experimental branch.
